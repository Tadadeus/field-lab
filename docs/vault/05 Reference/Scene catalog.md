## Fluid Lab (`fl`), 28 scenes
Lab note: [[Lab Fluid]]

### Wind tunnel

| key | title | what it shows |
|---|---|---|
| `fl_karman` | Kármán vortex street behind a cylinder | Above Re ≈ 47 the twin vortices behind a cylinder break loose and shed alternately, top, bottom, top: a von Kármán street. The lift wobbles at the shedding frequency, and the Strouhal number St = fD/U settles near 0.2 for a wide range of Re. |
| `fl_low` | Slow flow: two quiet vortices | At low Reynolds number the wake is steady: two counter-rotating vortices sit behind the cylinder and never leave. Raise Re past about 47 and they start to shed. |
| `fl_fast` | Fast flow: a turbulent wake | At high Reynolds number the wake turns into a chaotic, turbulent jumble of eddies of many sizes. An eddy viscosity (Smagorinsky model) stands in for eddies too small for the grid. |
| `fl_square` | Square cylinder | Sharp corners fix where the flow separates, so a square sheds a little more regularly than a circle and has a higher drag coefficient (about 2 compared with about 1.3). |
| `fl_plate` | Flat plate across the flow | A plate broadside to the flow is the bluffest body of all: huge separated vortices and a drag coefficient around 2. |
| `fl_wing` | Wing at 6°: lift | The flow speeds up over the top of the wing and slows below it, so the pressure is lower on top: lift. Watch Cl in the chart and the speed colours around the wing. |
| `fl_stall` | Wing at 22°: stall | Past about 15° the flow cannot follow the curved upper surface any more: it separates, big vortices roll off the top and the lift collapses while drag shoots up. That is a stall. |
| `fl_two` | Two cylinders side by side | Two wakes interact: depending on the gap they shed in phase, out of phase or flip-flop between a wide and a narrow wake. |

### Hypersurfaces in 4D

| key | title | what it shows |
|---|---|---|
| `cx_h_s3` | A 3-sphere, slice by slice | The 3-sphere x² + y² + z² + w² = 1 lives in four dimensions. Each slice w = constant is an ordinary sphere of radius √(1 − w²): it grows from a point, reaches radius 1 at w = 0, then shrinks away. |
| `cx_h_hyp1` | Hyperboloid: slices that only grow | x² + y² + z² − w² = c: every slice is a sphere of radius √(c + w²), smallest at w = 0. This is the shape of space-like distances in relativity. |
| `cx_h_hyp2` | Two sheets: nothing, then two spheres | w² − x² − y² − z² = c is empty for small /w/ and appears suddenly as a point that grows into a sphere: the slice changes its topology. |
| `cx_h_cone` | The light cone of relativity | x² + y² + z² = w²: with w as time, each slice is a sphere of light spreading out at speed 1. At w = 0 it shrinks to a single point. |
| `cx_h_morse` | A saddle changes the slice | x² + y² − z² + w² = c: as w passes ±√c the slice switches from a one-sheet hyperboloid to two sheets through a double cone. This is a critical point of Morse theory, seen live. |
| `cx_h_ctor` | The Clifford torus | A thickened Clifford torus: the flat torus inside the 3-sphere. Its slices are pairs of tubes that join and split as w moves. |
| `cx_h_tiger` | The tiger | (√(x² + y²) − a)² + (√(z² + w²) − a)² = b², a torus made from two circles at once. Its slices are tori that pinch and split. |
| `cx_h_sphc` | A sphere times a line | x² + y² + z² = 1 for any w: a 4D cylinder. Every slice is the same sphere, and the colour shows the hypersurface does not tilt into w at all. |
| `cx_h_gyr` | A gyroid drifting through w | A triply periodic surface whose pattern slides as w changes, so the slices flow like a wave. |
| `cx_h_blobs` | Two 4D blobs meet | Two round lumps sit at different places in w. At w = 0 their slices touch and merge; on either side only one of them is seen. |
